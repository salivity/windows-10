Snipping Tool and Snip & Sketch in Windows 10

The Microsoft Windows 10 operating system includes two dedicated screen capture utilities: the legacy Snipping Tool and the modernized Snip & Sketch app. This article outlines the primary purpose of both tools, explains their screen capture and annotation capabilities, and clarifies why both applications exist simultaneously within Windows 10.

The Purpose of Snipping Tool

The Snipping Tool is a legacy Windows utility introduced in Windows Vista designed to capture still screenshots of an open window, rectangular area, free-form shape, or the entire screen. Its primary purpose is to provide a quick, simple way to capture on-screen information without needing third-party software or relying on the standard Print Screen keyboard key, which captures the entire desktop to the clipboard.

Key functions of the Snipping Tool include:

  • Capture Modes: Free-form, rectangular, window, and full-screen snips.
  • Delay Timer: A delay feature (from 1 to 5 seconds) allowing users to open menus or pop-ups before the screen capture initiates.
  • Basic Markup: A basic pen and highlighter tool to draw over captures, along with an eraser to remove annotations.
  • Export Options: Direct saving to formats such as PNG, JPEG, GIF, or single-file HTML (MHT), as well as direct email forwarding.

The Purpose of Snip & Sketch

Snip & Sketch was introduced in the Windows 10 October 2018 Update as a modern Universal Windows Platform (UWP) replacement for the Snipping Tool. Its purpose is to streamline the screen capture workflow, integrate seamlessly with modern Windows shortcuts, and offer touch-friendly, pen-optimized editing tools.

Key enhancements provided by Snip & Sketch include:

  • Global Shortcut Integration: Native activation via the Windows Key + Shift + S keyboard combination, which invokes a lightweight overlay without opening the full application window.
  • Expanded Annotation Tools: Addition of pencil tools, a virtual ruler, a protractor for measuring angles, and a cropping tool.
  • Windows Ink Support: Optimized for digital stylus and touch-screen devices, allowing precise handwriting and pressure-sensitive drawing.
  • Streamlined Sharing: Integration with the native Windows 10 Share menu to quickly send captures to apps, email, or nearby devices.

Why Both Exist in Windows 10

The presence of both applications in Windows 10 represents a transitional phase in Microsoft's operating system development. Microsoft intended to deprecate the older Snipping Tool in favor of Snip & Sketch's modern architecture and keyboard-friendly overlay. However, to maintain backward compatibility, support enterprise environments, and prevent disruption for users accustomed to the legacy interface, Microsoft kept the original Snipping Tool active alongside Snip & Sketch throughout the life cycle of Windows 10.
