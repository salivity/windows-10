Configure Audio Balance and Spatial Sound in Windows 10

Windows 10 includes native controls that allow users to fine-tune speaker output levels and enable immersive 3D audio. Adjusting the audio balance helps correct hearing imbalances or asymmetrical speaker placement by shifting volume between the left and right channels. Meanwhile, spatial sound enhancements simulate a three-dimensional audio environment using formats like Windows Sonic for Headphones or Dolby Atmos. This guide explains the exact steps required to configure both audio balance and spatial sound settings directly within Windows 10.

Configuring Audio Balance in Windows 10

To adjust the volume balance between your left and right audio channels:

  1. Open Sound Settings: Right-click the speaker icon in the system tray (bottom-right corner of the taskbar) and select Open Sound settings, or navigate to Start > Settings > System > Sound.
  2. Select Device Properties: Under the Output section, ensure your active playback device is selected in the dropdown menu, then click Device properties.
  3. Adjust the Balance Sliders: In the Device properties window, locate the Balance section. You will see sliders labeled L (Left) and R (Right). Drag the sliders independently to shift the audio bias toward one channel or balance uneven outputs.

Alternatively, you can configure balance via the legacy Control Panel:

  1. Press Windows Key + R, type mmsys.cpl, and press Enter.
  2. In the Playback tab, double-click your active audio device.
  3. Go to the Levels tab and click the Balance button.
  4. Adjust the numeric values for the individual channels and click OK.

Configuring Spatial Sound Enhancements

Spatial sound provides an enhanced, virtualized surround sound experience, particularly when using stereo headphones. Windows 10 supports formats including the built-in Windows Sonic, as well as third-party solutions like Dolby Atmos for Headphones and DTS Headphone:X.

Method 1: Via the Taskbar (Quick Access)

  1. Right-click the speaker icon in the system tray.
  2. Hover over Spatial sound.
  3. Select your preferred provider, such as Windows Sonic for Headphones.

Method 2: Via Windows Settings

  1. Go to Settings > System > Sound.
  2. Under Output, click Device properties.
  3. Locate the Spatial sound section at the bottom of the page.
  4. Open the dropdown menu under Spatial sound format and choose Windows Sonic for Headphones (or an installed third-party format).

Method 3: Via Control Panel (Advanced Properties)

  1. Press Windows Key + R, type mmsys.cpl, and press Enter.
  2. Select your playback device and click Properties.
  3. Click on the Spatial sound tab.
  4. Select your desired spatial sound format from the dropdown menu.
  5. If using headphones, check the box labeled Turn on 7.1 virtual surround sound if it is available for your format.
  6. Click Apply, then OK to save the changes.
